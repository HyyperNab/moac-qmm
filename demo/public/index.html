// KERNEL ARCHITECTURE

How the tensor collision works

Traditional models ask: “What does this drug do to the average patient?” The kernel asks: “Can THIS exact biological state survive every dimension at once?”

01

No average patients

A CYP2C19 *17/*17 ultra-rapid metabolizer gets 35% PPI efficacy on a standard dose. The genomic tensor hardcodes metabolic reality — and rare genotypes thin the Bayesian evidence base for “average patient” assumptions.

02

Everything collides at once

Hypoxia derates CYP450. Albumin displacement spikes free fractions. The microbiome eats your drug. Sleep debt kills glymphatic clearance. 25 phases compute every interaction simultaneously — an ODE here, a Monte Carlo there, a Nash equilibrium in between.

03

Kill-switches, not averages

GFR < 30 → CLEARANCE-KILL. IL-6 > 80 and TNF-α > 50 → CYTOKINE-STORM. Two organs failing → CROSSTALK-KILL. When a biological constraint is violated, the engine aborts with the exact reason — it never averages its way past reality.

04

The pathogen plays chess

The v28 payoff matrix has zero pure Nash equilibria: against a strategic pathogen there is no stable deterministic treatment. The kernel computes minimax, learns payoffs from outcomes (adaptive matrix) and pre-trains a Q-learning agent before recommending.
