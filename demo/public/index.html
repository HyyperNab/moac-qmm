// HOW IT WORKS — AND WHY USE IT

A digital rehearsal for a drug protocol

In real patients, a drug never acts alone. Its fate depends on the patient's genome, kidney and liver, albumin level, sleep quality, gut bacteria, even the time of day it is taken. Textbook dosing assumes a healthy "average patient" — and that average does not exist. This engine lets you rehearse a protocol against a full patient profile before reality does the experiment for you.

01 — WHAT IT DOES

Two inputs, one verdict

You provide two things: a patient profile — CYP450 genetics, kidney and liver function, albumin, organ states, sleep architecture, toxins, microbiome — and a drug protocol — dose, interval, protein binding, metabolism pathway, receptor affinity, therapeutic window. The engine collides them through 25 simultaneous biological checks and returns one signed result: the protocol survived, or it aborted with the named reason.

02 — HOW IT THINKS

Everything interacts at once

Low albumin doesn't just lower albumin — it spikes the unbound fraction of your drug. Hypoxia doesn't just lower oxygen — it derates the very liver enzymes clearing the drug. The engine models these couplings explicitly: a Monte-Carlo simulation decides receptor engagement, a differential equation integrates the immune response, a degradation graph propagates organ failure, and a game-theory matrix checks whether a pathogen could outmaneuver the treatment.

03 — HONEST FAILURE

It refuses instead of averaging

When a hard biological limit is breached — kidney function below the safe floor, a cytokine storm brewing, two drugs waging war on the same albumin — the engine aborts and names the constraint. It never averages its way past a limit. That is the feature: you learn the specific bottleneck your protocol hits, instead of a reassuring number that hides it.

04 — WHY USE IT

Build intuition safely

Use it to explore, teach and stress-test: see how a CYP2C19 ultra-rapid metabolizer loses 65% of a standard PPI dose; how hypoalbuminemia turns a "normal" dose into a supra-therapeutic one; how once-daily dosing fails when the simulated half-life is 20 minutes. It is a reasoning sandbox for pharmacology — never a dosing tool. All findings are model output from unverified assumptions.

// LIVE DEMO — REAL KERNEL, SERVERLESS

Collide a patient

The real Python engine runs in a serverless function — same physics and constants as the GitHub package. The built-in profiles simulate esomeprazole (a PPI) in two very different patients. To simulate any other drug — valproate, warfarin, an antibiotic — select “Custom JSON” and paste your drug protocol into the editor that appears in the left panel. Fields you omit fall back to safe schema defaults.
